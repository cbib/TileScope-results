# Human GBM: interpretation of the selected spatial examples

The numerical analysis covers 30 IDH-wildtype sections, 91 program pairs and
192 ROIs. The illustrated examples were selected after examining the full
inventory; they are exploratory, not predeclared confirmatory comparisons.
The published program definitions are from Greenwald et al.; the sections
include both Greenwald and Ravi cohorts.

## Examples used in Figure 3

**259_T ROI 2:** AC, Mac and Reactive-Ast share an enriched territory, whereas
MES-Hyp is depleted there. Reactive-Ast correlates positively with AC and Mac
and negatively with MES-Hyp: spot/ensemble values are +0.14/+0.56,
+0.11/+0.49 and −0.12/−0.43, respectively. The broader organisation is clearer
after aggregation; the modest spot coefficients should not be described as
strong coupling. No ROI 3→2 boundary measurement is present for this example.

**262_T, NPC–Neuron:** both programs are enriched in ROI 5 (3.05/5.84;
joint-high 4.95), but correlate little within it (+0.046/+0.059 at
spots/ensemble). ROI 4 has weaker enrichment (1.80/1.35) but stronger
correlation (+0.332/+0.754). Its two retained components contain 292 and 179
spots, with positive distance profiles that decay differently. Shared
territory does not require correlated variation; the maps do not establish
one smooth gradient spanning disconnected components.

**260_T, MES-Ast–MES-Hyp:** in responder ROI 2, MES-Hyp scores are higher near
the ROI 4 boundary than farther within ROI 2 (+0.600/+0.309 at spots/ensemble).
MES-Ast differences are nearly zero (−0.009/−0.003), as are the pair's
correlations (−0.030/−0.050). The forward comparison has 272 near and 366 far
spots; the reverse measures ROI 4 and has 216/34. These ordered comparisons
measure boundary-localised expression, not signalling or transport.
Filtering contacts by enrichment at both endpoints would omit this example.

## Cohort frequencies

Neural/glial and reactive–myeloid pairs often share territories and have
positive local correlations; selected MES-Hyp pairs more often oppose them.
This is a tendency, not a fixed classification of each pair. Frequencies are
means of within-section fractions of evaluable local units, with equal section
weights. They are not fractions of positive patients or averages of signed
correlations. The complete coefficients and denominators are in Supplementary
Data 2.

Source/ensemble availability differs (185/168 evaluable ROIs per pair);
matched-ROI comparisons avoid attributing denominator changes to aggregation.
Descriptive bands change the reported percentages. Same-ROI persistence across
four supports is a separate query, not a conjunction of marginal frequencies.
Likewise, requiring a distance coefficient to exceed a band in every shell
can miss a relationship that decays with distance.

## Additional exploratory observations

The authored records cover 20 paired source/consensus views and 27 local
assessments. They remain in `batch_01_observations.json` and
`shortlist_vetting/observations.json`; the exact boundary image cited in the
record is preserved in `review_witnesses/`. Rebuilding tables does not repeat
the visual assessment.

- 270_T ROI 4, inspected with MES–Oligo, is dispersed through ROI 2:
  40 spots, 33 components, largest four. Lack of a consensus statistic does
  not erase the observed population. This arrangement does not prove invasion.
- The 19-spot ROI 7 inclusion in 248_T ROI 9 is geometry-only for the inspected
  pair. Whole-parent enrichment cannot be attributed to that one inclusion.
- 262_T AC–MES-Hyp ROI 1 and 243_T AC–MES-Hyp ROI 4 show substantial
  spot/consensus disagreement. No new seed-sensitivity evidence is implied.
- Positive correlation need not indicate enriched overlap: in 248_T AC–OPC
  ROI 2, AC is depleted (0.57) despite correlation +0.470/+0.862.

These selected examples do not estimate the prevalence of tissue arrangements.
Negative correlation alone cannot distinguish separated patches from boundary
bands, and neither morphology nor score correlation establishes a mechanism.
